Kyoto After
dark

Five days in Kyoto built around the quiet hours: temples after the tour groups leave, lantern-lit lanes in Gion, and nights in a ryokan with a hot bath and a kaiseki dinner.

Duration
5 days
Budget
Set by you
Best season
March to May, October to November
Stay
Traditional ryokan

The journey

Kyoto has two faces, the crowded daytime city and the quiet evening one. We plan your days around the second, with long rests in the afternoon and temple visits timed for the late, low light.

You stay in a small ryokan with futon beds, a bath and a kaiseki dinner served at the inn. Almost everything else is a short walk or taxi ride away.

Good to know

Rain plan
Kyoto is lovely in light rain and the temples are quieter for it. On wet days Arashiyama and the tea ceremony take the place of the open-air walks.
Seasonal lights
Evening illuminations at some temples run only in spring and autumn. We confirm the dates when we plan your trip.

Day by day

The
itinerary

  1. Day 01

    Arrive and settle

    Transfer to your ryokan, tea in the room, a soak in the bath, and your first kaiseki dinner.

    Indoor
  2. Day 02

    Fushimi Inari at dusk

    The torii gates of Fushimi Inari after the day-trippers have gone. The path stays open all night, so you can walk as far as you like. Late ramen near the station.

    Outdoor
  3. Day 03

    Higashiyama and Gion

    Kiyomizu-dera and the stone lanes of Higashiyama in the late afternoon, then an evening walk through Gion and dinner on Pontocho.

    Outdoor
  4. Day 04

    Arashiyama, then tea

    The bamboo grove and Tenryu-ji early, a calm afternoon with a private tea ceremony, and a quiet night at the ryokan.

    Mixed
  5. Day 05

    Nijo Castle and departure

    Nijo Castle and its nightingale floors when it opens, then your transfer to the airport.

    Indoor
